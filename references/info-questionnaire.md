# Discovery Questionnaire (Phase A)

Gather as much relevant context as possible before making a project decision. **Conduct at least three rounds of dialogue**, rather than delivering every question in a single batch. Use each answer to shape the next round. Follow up when answers are vague or slogan-like.

## Required Three-Round Structure

### Round 1: Facts — What Is the Project?

Choose the 6–8 most relevant questions from the 12 dimensions below. Establish what is being done, for whom, by when, with what budget and team, from which starting point, and toward what definition of success.

**Do not move directly to Phase B after this round.**

### Round 2: Constraints and Motivations — Why, and Within What Limits?

Ask 5–7 questions. Most must respond to specific Round 1 answers, rather than repeat a generic questionnaire. Include:

- For each firm requirement: Why this standard? Who set it? What happens if it is not met?
- Non-negotiable limits and fears: Which outcome is most concerning? What would make abandoning the project preferable? Examples include violating a requirement or spending money without a useful result.
- Previous attempts: What has already been tried, and why did it not work?
- Stakeholders: Who would oppose the project, who would benefit, and who decides?
- Resources: What is already available, and what is missing?

### Round 3: Stress Testing — What If an Assumption Fails?

Ask 5–7 questions that expose `[Unknown]` items. Include:

- Challenge the most confident assumption: "What if that assumption does not hold?" For example, what if the expected audience growth does not happen within the planned period?
- Establish the source of key numbers: Are budget, timeline, and targets evidence-based estimates or guesses? What supports them?
- Identify the weakest link: Which part is least certain?
- Test reversibility: Which mistakes can be undone, and which cannot?
- Establish priorities: What is cut first if money or time runs short?
- Test partial success: If only half the goal can be achieved, which half is acceptable?

Produce the project fact sheet after the third round.

## General Principles

- Collect only decision-relevant information. Prefer role labels, aliases, and ranges over names, contact details, and precise private figures when those details would not change the decision.
- Never request credentials or secrets for this questionnaire. Record a redacted summary rather than copying unnecessary sensitive disclosures into the fact sheet or archive.
- Ask the 5–8 most important questions per round; do not overwhelm the user with a huge questionnaire.
- It is acceptable for the user not to know. Record `[Unknown]`, mark it for research or validation, and do not invent an answer.
- Remember volunteered information and do not ask for it again.
- Translate slogans such as "do it well," "make money," or "grow the audience" into specific numbers and situations.
- If the user asks to stop questioning, stop early and list unexamined dimensions as `[Unknown]`. If the user stops the whole task, stop the task.

## Dimension 1: Goals and Motivations

- What must this project achieve? Describe a verifiable end state in one sentence.
- Why do it? What need, concern, or opportunity drives it? Ask why twice to reach the underlying motivation.
- Is it necessary, advisable, or simply desirable? What happens if it is not done?
- What is the minimum successful version? What is the ideal version?
- Who will judge the outcome successful, and on what basis?

## Dimension 2: Scope and Boundaries

- Explicitly list what is included and excluded.
- What form will the deliverable take: a document, product, contract, system, action, or something else?
- When are results needed? Is there a hard deadline?
- What geographic, audience, platform, and legal boundaries apply?

## Dimension 3: Stakeholders

- Who benefits, who is disadvantaged, who decides, and who can veto?
- Who executes, who assists, and who receives the final deliverable or report?
- Are there overlooked stakeholders: people, teams, or systems affected but not represented?

## Dimension 4: Resources

- What is the budget ceiling? Where does funding come from? What happens if costs exceed it?
- Who contributes, and how much time can they give? Are vendors or contractors involved?
- How many work hours or weeks are available? What are the milestones?
- What tools, data, templates, channels, relationships, and technical capabilities already exist?

## Dimension 5: Constraints and Non-Negotiable Limits

- What hard constraints must be respected: laws, regulations, contracts, organizational requirements, physical limits, or a fixed budget?
- What is off limits: changing a protected system, disrupting a relationship, or disclosing confidential information?
- Which decisions are reversible? Which cannot be undone once made?

## Dimension 6: Current State and Starting Point

- Where does the project stand? What is complete, and where is it blocked?
- Is there baseline data? What is the gap between the current state and the goal?
- Has anyone attempted something similar? What succeeded or failed, and why?

## Dimension 7: Risks and Failure Modes

- What is the worst plausible outcome? How likely is it, and is it tolerable?
- Where do problems already seem likely?
- Which implausible approaches might still be proposed?

## Dimension 8: Success Criteria and Measurement

- Which metrics, acceptance criteria, or checklists determine success?
- Which intermediate checkpoints can reveal problems early?
- How often should progress be reviewed?

## Dimension 9: Alternatives and Existing Preferences

- Does the user already prefer an approach? Which one, and why?
- Which options have already been rejected? Record the reasons so they can be checked later.
- Are there useful examples or benchmarks from comparable projects?

## Dimension 10: Uncertainty and Unknowns

- What are the one to three least certain aspects?
- Which missing facts must be researched, asked about, or tested before proceeding?
- Does the plan depend on taking a chance? What exactly is being wagered?

## Dimension 11: History and Context

- Which organizational politics, conventions, technical debt, or legacy issues affect the decision?
- Which previous commitments or relationships constrain this decision?

## Dimension 12: Authority and Responsibility

- Who makes the final decision? How much authority does the user have?
- Who is accountable if the decision is wrong? What approvals are required?
- Will the resulting workflow be executed directly or submitted for approval?
